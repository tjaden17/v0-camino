## The Short Answer

Camino grows through three compounding mechanisms that each feed the next:

  1. Product-led individual virality — users share signals externally, receivers want the product
  2. Group-led step-change growth — one coordinator brings 8-200 orgs in a single event
  3. Data network effects — more orgs make the product better for all orgs, making switching irrational

These three mechanisms are not independent. Each one strengthens the others.
A user who shares a signal (mechanism 1) may share with a board member who coordinates
a portfolio (mechanism 2), whose portfolio companies contribute to the cross-org benchmark
graph (mechanism 3), which makes the signal view more valuable to share (back to mechanism 1).

---

## Phase 1: Direct Sales + Product Foundation (Now to 20 Customers)

How growth happens: Founder-led direct sales. You find customers, close them manually.

What you are building during this phase:
- The core product that makes customers successful enough to share
- The shareable signal view — the primitive that every viral loop requires
- The data model that supports group membership and org connections (even if unused yet)

Why this phase matters for future growth: Every customer you onboard manually is a
potential group coordinator later. Surge from Locumate could become the anchor of
an advisor network. Treat every early customer as a future distribution node.

Signal that this phase is working: Customers voluntarily show Camino to someone else,
unprompted, without a referral incentive.

---

## Phase 2: Viral Loops Activate (20 to 100 Customers)

How growth happens: A meaningful fraction of new customers come from shares and referrals.
Direct sales still required but CAC starts declining.

What activates in this phase:
- Shared signal views being opened by non-customers (Loop 1)
- First advisor or investor connections (early group formation)
- K coefficient reaches approximately 0.1-0.15

What you need to measure:
- How many share events happen per month
- What percentage of share link opens convert to sign-ups
- Which roles are most likely to share (CEOs? Advisors? Investors?)

Signal that this phase is working: You can attribute at least 20% of new signups to
a share or referral event, without offering a referral incentive.

---

## Phase 3: Group Formation Accelerates Growth (100 to 500 Customers)

How growth happens: Group coordinators (investors, advisors, accelerators, franchisors)
bring their entire networks onto Camino in single events. Each event adds 8-200 orgs.

What activates in this phase:
- Advisor multi-org dashboard — free tier for advisors who bring 2+ clients
- Investor portfolio view — pitched directly to investors as a portfolio monitoring tool
- Accelerator partnerships — Camino as the default analytics tool for cohort members

Why this is a step-change: A single accelerator partnership with a cohort of 20 startups
doubles your customer count if you are at 20 customers. At 100 customers it adds 20%.
The absolute number is the same — the relative impact diminishes as you scale, which is
why you want to activate group formation as early as possible.

Signal that this phase is working: At least one group coordinator event per month.
Average orgs per group event greater than 5.

---

## Phase 4: Data Network Effects Create a Moat (500+ Customers)

How growth happens: The product becomes measurably better than any competitor for any
new customer, because 500 orgs have contributed signal patterns to the cross-org graph.

What activates in this phase:
- Industry-specific benchmarks emerge — "you are in the top quartile for B2B SaaS at
  your deal size and stage"
- Predictive signals — "response time increases of this magnitude have historically
  preceded NPS drops within 3 weeks in businesses like yours"
- Cross-group benchmarks — portfolio companies benchmarked against accelerator cohort
  companies at the same stage

Why this is a moat: A competitor launching today starts with zero cross-org data.
To replicate your benchmark accuracy they need 500 orgs and 12 months of signal history.
That is a 2-year head start that compounds — by the time they catch up, you have 2000 orgs.

Signal that this phase is working: Benchmark data is cited by customers as a primary
reason they chose Camino over alternatives.

## The One Metric That Predicts All of This

Time to first share event per customer.

If a customer shares a signal view within their first 30 days, the product has delivered
enough value to create a social proof moment. If they never share in 90 days, they are
using Camino as a private tool and will churn when something cheaper comes along.

Track this from day one. Optimise the product experience to create a natural share moment
within the first week — ideally triggered by a signal insight that surprises them.

## What Could Break This Model

1. The shared view is not good enough to make a recipient want the product.
   Fix: Invest heavily in the design and content quality of the shareable view.
   This is a design problem before it is a product problem.

2. Group coordinators do not see enough value in the coordinator role.
   Fix: The coordinator view must be significantly more valuable than a member view.
   If the investor's portfolio dashboard is not genuinely useful, they will not bring
   their portfolio.

3. Cross-org benchmark data is too thin or too generic to be believable.
   Fix: Do not show benchmarks until you have statistical confidence. Showing a benchmark
   based on 4 companies is worse than showing no benchmark.

4. Customers perceive data sharing as a privacy risk.
   Fix: Anonymisation must be visible and explicit. Every benchmark screen must say
   "based on X anonymised businesses — your data is never identifiable to others."

## Summary

Camino grows by making each customer a distribution node — through shares, through
group membership, and through contributing to a data graph that makes the product
better for everyone. The product is the sales motion. The data is the moat.
The groups are the accelerant.
